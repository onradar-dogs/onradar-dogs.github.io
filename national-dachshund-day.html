    When Is National Dachshund Day and Why June 21?

    National Dachshund Day is celebrated on June 21. The Awareness Days calendar entry lists it as Sunday, June 21, 2026, Monday, June 21, 2027 and Wednesday, June 21, 2028, and sums up the reason in one line: the longest dog on the longest day of the year.

    Who started it?

    Nobody officially. The same calendar notes that the exact origins are unclear and that the day grew out of online dachshund communities. It may have evolved from “Dachshunds on Parade,” an event that began around 2000 as a class project at Central Washington University and was held on the third Saturday of June. That grassroots origin is why you will not find an official proclamation — and why every owner is free to celebrate in their own way.

    Is it the same as other dachshund days?

    Owners sometimes mix up National Dachshund Day with breed meetups and seasonal races held on other dates. June 21 is the one most widely used online. On r/Dachshund, a “Happy Dachshund Day!” post on June 21, 2026 drew more than 2,000 upvotes, with owners trading photos of their dogs sunbathing and doing the upright “meerkat” pose — and one commenter joking that the date matches the solstice because dachshunds are solar-powered.

    Where Does the Dachshund Come From?

    The dachshund is a German hunting dog, and its name says so: Dachs means badger and Hund means dog. It was bred to follow badgers into their burrows, which explains the long body, deep chest, strong digging paws and surprisingly loud bark.

    How old the breed is depends on who you ask. According to Wikipedia’s dachshund article, the AKC claims the breed goes back to the 15th century, the Dachshund Club of America places its first descriptions in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the earliest verifiable written references date to the early 1700s.

    Three coats, two sizes

    Dachshunds come in smooth, longhaired and wirehaired coats. The wirehaired coat was the last to develop, in the late 19th century. In the US there are two official sizes: standard, typically 16–32 lb, and miniature, up to about 11 lb. “Tweenie” is an owners’ nickname for dogs in between, not an official size.

    From royal courts to the Olympics

    Royal households across Europe kept dachshunds, and Queen Victoria was particularly fond of the breed. The American Kennel Club recognized the dachshund in 1885. During World War I the breed’s German image hurt its popularity in the US, and owners started calling their dogs “liberty hounds.” The comeback was complete by 1972, when a striped dachshund named Waldi became the first official Olympic mascot at the Munich Games. Picasso, Andy Warhol and David Hockney all lived with dachshunds too — the full story is in our timeline of the dachshund in pop culture.

    What Is a Dachshund’s Personality Really Like?

    Bold, loyal, clever and stubborn. Dachshunds were bred to make decisions underground without a handler, so they think for themselves — which is charming at a party and challenging during recall training.

    Expect a dog that attaches strongly to its people, often picks a favorite, can be reserved with strangers and barks with a big-dog voice. Most love to dig, burrow under blankets and follow a scent trail. Our full profile of dachshund temperament and personality goes deeper; for celebration planning, the key point is that a dachshund enjoys a day that uses its nose and keeps its people close far more than a loud, crowded one.

    How Can You Celebrate National Dachshund Day?

    The best celebrations match your dog’s temperament: sniffing games for explorers, meetups for social dogs and cozy enrichment for homebodies. Here are ideas that work whether June 21 falls on a weekend or a workday.

    Ideas for explorers

    • A “sniffari” walk. Let your dog choose the route and stop as often as it likes. A slow, sniff-heavy walk gives a scent hound plenty of mental work.
    • Backyard scent trail. Drag a treat across the grass in a zigzag and hide it at the end.
    • Find-it cups. Hide a treat under one of three cups, then add more cups as your dog gets the idea.

    Ideas for social butterflies

    • A dachshund meetup. Pick a fenced park, set a one-hour window and invite owners through local breed groups. Small, calm groups suit the breed better than big crowds.
    • A fun race. Dachshund races are a beloved tradition. In Cincinnati, the Running of the Wieners opens Oktoberfest Zinzinnati; CityBeat’s report on the 2019 race describes 100 dachshunds in hot-dog-bun wraps, with a longhaired dog named Maple taking first place. A home version needs only a short, flat, grassy lane and owners waiting at the end with treats.
    • Costume photo walk. Keep costumes light, short-term and easy to remove.

    Ideas for homebodies

    • Blanket burrow upgrade. Dachshunds love to tunnel. A fresh fleece blanket or burrow bed is a gift they will use every day.
    • Frozen lick mat or food puzzle for a slow, calm afternoon treat.
    • Five-minute trick session. Teach “spin” or “paw” with tiny rewards; short, upbeat sessions work best with this independent breed — see training tips for stubborn dachshunds.

    Ideas that help other dachshunds

    • Donate to or volunteer with a breed-specific rescue.
    • Share an adoptable dachshund’s profile from a local shelter.
    • Offer to walk a neighbor’s dog if they cannot get out that day.

    Your National Dachshund Day Checklist

    1. Save the date: Monday, June 21, 2027 (after that, Wednesday, June 21, 2028).
    2. Pick one activity that matches your dog’s style (use the planner above).
    3. Check the weather: June can be hot. Walk early or late, bring water and choose shade for meetups — paved paths heat up fast for a low-slung dog.
    4. Plan the route or venue and confirm dogs are allowed.
    5. Prepare treats in advance and count them into your dog’s daily food.
    6. Charge your phone for photos, and agree on a hashtag with friends.
    7. Build in rest: a quiet hour at home after any outing.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid on the Day

    • Too many dogs at once. A big, noisy crowd can overwhelm a dog that is reserved with strangers. Keep meetups small.
    • Treat overload. Everyone wants to spoil the birthday-style guest. Bring your own treats and ask others not to feed your dog.
    • Midday heat. Long walks at noon in late June are hard on any small dog.
    • Costumes that restrict movement. If your dog freezes, scratches or walks oddly, take it off.
    • Turning a race serious. Short, flat and fun — no ramps, jumps or long sprints. Our page on dachshund exercise requirements explains what everyday activity suits the breed.
