Dachshund Harness and Coat Size Chart Compared: Mini vs Tweenie vs Standard Across Brands

Size labels mean different things at every brand. Compare real dachshund harness and coat charts side by side, learn where to measure a long back, and get a starting size from your own measurements.

    A dachshund harness and coat size chart only helps if you know which brand it belongs to — because a “Medium” or a “Tweenie” at one company is not the same dog at the next. Long backs and deep chests break generic pet sizing, and with winter coat season already here, ordering the wrong size now can mean waiting through a return while the cold walks start. Measure once, properly, and use the comparison below to pick a size you are unlikely to send back.

    Quick answer

    Take three numbers with the dog standing: chest girth at the deepest part of the ribcage, back length (write down whether you started at the base of the neck or between the shoulder blades) and neck. Harnesses are sized mainly by chest; dachshund coats are sized by back length first, then chest. Size labels like Mini, Tweenie and Standard are only shortcuts — when you are between sizes, the brands below say go up (harness) or pick the longer back length (coat).

    Why Don’t Normal Dog Size Charts Fit Dachshunds?

    Most dog clothing assumes that a dog with a long back also has a wide chest, and a dachshund breaks that rule in both directions. The result is the complaint you see again and again from owners: a coat fits the chest or the length, never both.

    That exact frustration is the opening line of a 2026 r/Dachshund thread on clothes that actually fit. Replies add two practical rules of thumb: if a jacket has leg sleeves it is unlikely to fit a barrel chest, and the belly area of a made-to-measure fleece can still hang loose when only length and chest were sent to the maker.

    The size names are not official

    Mini and Standard are show classes; Tweenie is not. Wikipedia’s dachshund article gives the standard as typically 16–32 lb (7.3–14.5 kg) and the US miniature as up to about 11 lb, and describes “tweenie” as an informal owners’ term for dogs between the two, not an official classification. The FCI also recognizes a smaller rabbit-size (kaninchen) dachshund of about 8–11 lb, and some clubs, including Germany’s, measure chest circumference to separate sizes. So when a brand says “fits tweenies,” it means “fits dogs that fall between our Mini and Standard rows” — nothing more precise.

    Weight is the weakest clue

    Two dogs of the same weight can need different coats: a lean, long standard and a stocky, short-backed mini can both weigh 15 lb. Weight is useful for narrowing a harness range; it should never be the only number you send to a shop.

    How Do You Measure a Dachshund for a Harness or Coat?

    Measure chest girth around the deepest part of the ribcage, back length along the spine with the dog standing straight, and the neck where a collar sits. Use a soft tape, or a piece of string you then hold against a ruler.

    This 36-second video from a UK dachshund harness maker shows the neck and chest measurements, the “size up if between sizes” rule and the two-finger fit check, which is easier to understand when you see it on a real dog:

    Chest girth: find the true deepest point

    Most charts say “just behind the front legs.” Dachshunds often carry their deepest point a little further back. The author of a dachshund owner’s Hurtta sizing guide recommends measuring the true widest part of the chest, because it is not always right behind the legs. Slide the tape a little backward and forward and keep the biggest number.

    Back length: the start point changes the size

    This is where most cross-brand mistakes happen. Brands do not agree where back length begins:

    • Base of the neck to the base of the tail — the method in the Dachshund Fashionista size guide.
    • Between the shoulder blades to 1 cm before the tail — the method in the Charming Couture measurement guide, which also says to round up half centimeters.
    • Withers (between the shoulder blades) to the base of the tail — the method the Hurtta-sizing blogger uses for her own dogs.

    A neck-base measurement is always longer than a shoulder-blade measurement on the same dog. Take both and label them, and you can use any chart without re-measuring a wriggling dog.

    Neck: last, but not optional

    Dachshunds often have a neck that is small compared with their chest. A harness with separate neck and chest adjustment avoids a loose neck loop on a tight chest strap.

    What the comparison shows

    • “Tweenie” overlaps both neighbors. Django puts tweenies and smaller standards in the same Medium, while Fashionista splits tweenies into a narrow-chest 15 S and a stockier 15 M that also fits shorter standards.
    • Chest ranges are much wider than back steps. Fashionista’s 14-inch back alone spans chests from 14 to 23 inches across its S, M and L — proof that back length and chest need to be checked separately.
    • Charming Couture sizes almost purely by back length, in 3 cm steps, with small and medium chest versions at some lengths. If its calculator returns more than one match, the guide says to choose the size with the longest back.

    How Should a Dachshund Harness Fit?

    Snug enough that the dog cannot back out, loose enough to slide two fingers under the straps, with the chest panel sitting on the breastbone rather than across the shoulders.

    Django’s guidance is to size from chest girth and, if your dachshund falls between sizes, choose the larger size. The Bailey & Coco video adds a short walk test after fitting: watch that the straps do not rub behind the front legs and that the harness does not twist sideways when the dog turns.

    Escape artists need a different design, not a smaller size

    Going down a size to stop a dog slipping out usually leads to rubbing. Look instead for designs with a second strap further back on the body. We looked at one of these in our Houdini Weenie harness review, which explains how a dual-anchor fit holds a barrel-chested dachshund.

    Re-check the fit every season

    A winter coat under a harness adds bulk, and puppies change size quickly. Adjust straps when you swap coats, and plan to re-measure a growing puppy at least monthly — our dachshund puppy care guide covers the first months at home.

    How Should a Dachshund Coat Fit?

    A coat should end around the base of the tail, cover the deepest part of the chest, and fasten under the belly without gaping or pressing.

    Length matters more than owners expect. One r/Dachshund owner with a standard explained that a 35 cm Hurtta coat was slightly short and only worked with the back snap open, while 40 cm would have been right. On the blogger’s own small dogs, a 10.5 lb dog with a 13-inch back and 16.25-inch chest wears a Hurtta size 12, while a dog with a 14-inch back needed size 14 for the length even though her chest was smaller. For a reader’s dog with a 17-inch back and 23.5-inch chest, the blogger suggested size 18.

    Match the coat style to the body shape

    • Barrel chest: wrap styles with a belly panel and Velcro or clips usually beat pull-on jumpers. One owner whose dog’s front leg always got stuck in sleeves now buys only belly-fastening coats.
    • Long, lean back: choose by back length, then check the chest range fits; a longer size with an adjustable belly strap is often easier than a short one.
    • Long or wire coat: a dense coat already adds insulation, so a waterproof shell may be enough. See how the three dachshund coat types shed and how to groom each coat to keep fabric from matting long fur.

    Step-by-Step: Order the Right Size the First Time

    1. Measure standing, after a walk, when the dog is calm. One person holds a treat, one measures — the Charming Couture guide suggests exactly this for wiggly dogs.
    2. Take chest girth twice: behind the front legs and at the true deepest point. Keep the larger.
    3. Take back length twice: from the base of the neck and from between the shoulder blades, both to the base of the tail. Label them.
    4. Measure the neck where a collar sits.
    5. Read the chart for that exact product, not a shop’s general dog chart.
    6. Match the brand’s start point for back length and its priority (chest for harnesses, back for coats).
    7. Between sizes? For harnesses go up; for coats pick the longer back length and use the adjustable belly strap.
    8. Check returns before you buy, then do the two-finger and walk test indoors with tags on.

    Common Sizing Mistakes (and What They Cost You)

    • Ordering by label. “Tweenie” at one brand can be a Medium chest; at another it is a 15-inch back with two chest options.
    • Measuring a sitting or lying dog. Charming Couture is explicit: measure on all four legs.
    • Pulling the tape tight. You will order a size too small. Keep the tape flat and snug, not compressing the coat.
    • Using the same back length on every chart. A neck-base number entered into a shoulder-blade chart pushes you a size too long.
    • Waiting for the first frost. Ordering on the first cold morning leaves no time for an exchange if the size is wrong; measuring now does.
    • Forgetting exercise. A coat that restricts stride will be refused on longer walks — check movement against the walking you actually do, as covered in dachshund exercise requirements.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What size harness does a mini dachshund need?

    On Django’s Adventure harness chart, mini dachshunds and puppies usually fit Small, which covers a 13–16 inch (33–41 cm) chest. Always size by your dog’s chest girth rather than the Mini label.

    What size is a tweenie dachshund?

    Tweenie is an informal name for dogs between miniature and standard, not an official class. Django fits tweenies in Medium (16.5–20.5 inch chest); Dachshund Fashionista uses 15-inch back sizes, 15 S or 15 M depending on chest.

    Where do you measure a dachshund’s back length?

    It depends on the brand: some measure from the base of the neck to the base of the tail, others from between the shoulder blades to just before the tail. Measure both and use the one the chart specifies.

    Should I size up if my dachshund is between sizes?

    For harnesses, Django and the Bailey & Coco guide both say choose the larger size. For coats, Charming Couture recommends the size with the longer back length when more than one fits.

    How tight should a dachshund harness be?

    You should be able to slide two fingers under the straps, the dog should not be able to back out, and the harness should not rub behind the front legs during a short walk test.

    Why does my dachshund’s coat fit the chest but not the length?

    Dachshunds have long backs and deep chests in proportions general dog sizing does not expect. Choose a dachshund-specific coat sized by back length first, then confirm the chest range, ideally with an adjustable belly strap.

    The Bottom Line on Dachshund Sizing

    A good dachshund harness and coat size chart is brand-specific, so the best tool is your own set of labeled measurements: chest at the true deepest point, two back lengths and the neck. Measure this week, match each brand’s start point and priority, and you will spend the cold months walking instead of waiting on returns.
